## Unmetered vs unlimited: the two words do different work

Hosting vendors use both terms, and they do not mean the same thing.

OVHcloud's definition of an unmetered VPS is the cleanest one published: the volume of data transferred is not measured and has no limit, so you are not billed by how much your server sends or receives. In the same FAQ, OVHcloud answers whether bandwidth is truly unlimited with "yes, the traffic volume is unlimited. However, the maximum speed (bandwidth) of your network connection is set by the specific plan you choose."

Bluehost's editorial team splits the vocabulary in two. *Unmetered* means no charges for data usage but speeds capped by the provider. *Unlimited* means no limit on traffic volume or data transferred, subject to fair usage. Their own advice in the same piece: unlimited bandwidth doesn't guarantee maximum speed, and providers may throttle under heavy load.

Put those together and you get the rule that applies to every plan in the table above: **the meter is gone, but the ceiling moved to the port.** JustHost is unusually upfront about this, because it prints the port speed and the fair-share qualifier on the same line as the word UNMETERED. Most providers make you hunt for it.

### Why "93+ TB a month" appears next to the word unlimited

JustHost converts each port speed into a monthly volume: 100 Mbit becomes 31+ TB, 300 Mbit becomes 93+ TB, 500 Mbit becomes 155+ TB, 750 Mbit becomes 232+ TB.

Run the arithmetic yourself. A 300 Mbit port running flat out for 30 days moves roughly 97 TB. JustHost's 93+ TB figure is that same calculation with a little headroom shaved off. So the number is not an allowance you can exceed, it is the physical maximum that port can carry if it never idles. On a fair-share port you will not hit it consistently, because you are sharing the uplink. Treat 232 TB on a 750 Mbit plan as a theoretical ceiling, not a target.

## Five questions to settle before you pay

The word "unlimited" is not where providers hide things. The details around it are. These are the five you can check in a few minutes.

**1. Is traffic metered, and is overage billed or throttled?** OVHcloud's unmetered plans answer this cleanly: no metering, no overage charges, predictable monthly price. That is the good version. The bad version is a plan that advertises unlimited traffic, then bills per TB past a threshold buried in the terms.

**2. If you go over, what speed do you drop to?** This is where OVHcloud's Asia-Pacific region becomes the useful cautionary tale. OVHcloud excludes its Mumbai, Singapore and Sydney datacentres from the unlimited-traffic promise. On the SSD VPS pricing page, those locations carry a monthly quota of 500 GB for VPS-1, 1 TB for VPS-2 and VPS-3, and 3 TB for VPS-4, after which the port is capped at **10 Mbps**. Ten megabits is roughly a single 1080p stream. That is the difference between a usable server and a dead one, and it applies to three datacentres on an otherwise unlimited plan.

Worth noting: OVHcloud's separate unmetered-VPS page lists *different* quotas for the same VPS-1 through VPS-4 plans. Two pages, two sets of numbers, both live. Screenshot whatever you rely on.

**3. Is the port speed guaranteed or shared?** JustHost answers this with the word "fair-share" next to every port speed. Ask what it means for your plan at your location.

**4. Does your chosen datacentre have an exception?** The OVHcloud Asia-Pacific case is the pattern to watch for: unlimited in Frankfurt, quota-limited in Singapore. If you are buying for Asian or South American traffic, this is the question that decides the purchase.

**5. What does the acceptable-use policy say about your workload?** VPNs, proxies, streaming and file distribution are listed by OVHcloud as intended uses for its unmetered plans. Whether a given provider permits them is a contract question, not a bandwidth question. JustHost's plan pages advertise DDoS protection and a flexible constructor; the AUP itself is the document that governs what you may run.

## FAQ

**Is "unlimited traffic" on a VPS actually unlimited?**
The volume usually is not metered, but the port speed is capped by your plan. On JustHost that cap runs from 100 Mbit to 750 Mbit depending on what you buy.

**Will I be charged extra if I use more data than expected?**
On the plans above, no. The port speed is the limit, not a billable quota. Confirm this in the terms for whichever plan you pick, because not every provider structures it that way.

**Can I get a gigabit port on JustHost?**
Not according to its published plan table, which tops out at 750 Mbit. Reviewers of the Hong Kong and São Paulo locations describe 300 Mbit to 1 Gbit ports there, so the location you choose affects the ceiling.
